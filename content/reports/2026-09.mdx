# University AI Policy Dataset Month-End Report: September 2026

Dataset snapshot: `public-release-20260924-002`
Comparison baseline: `public-release-20260801-001`
Period: 1–30 September 2026
Status: Published with the combined site release

## Monthly result

The September public dataset contains 828 university records, 5,495
source-backed claims, 3,213 official source attributions, and 5,760 evidence
records. Compared with the August report snapshot, it adds 19 claims, 18 source
attributions, and 19 evidence records across 16 distinct university records.
University coverage remains unchanged at 828 records.

Two dataset releases were recorded during September: `public-release-20260921-001`
and `public-release-20260924-002`. The first added eight claims across six
university records; the second added eleven across eleven records. Pennsylvania
appears in both batches, so the monthly total is 16 distinct records, not 17.

These figures describe additions to the tracker, not 19 newly enacted policies.
Some September additions describe earlier institutional decisions: the Lund
guidelines took effect on 15 August, and the Universidad Adolfo Ibáñez policy
is dated 6 July. Retrieval, review, dataset publication, policy issuance, and
policy effective dates are separate events.

## Findings from September additions

### AI detection guidance has different institutional boundaries

The September additions include several distinct treatments of AI detection.
National Tsing Hua University's teaching guidance advises against using
third-party detection as the primary basis for determining student AI use.
Penn's teaching guidance tells faculty not to upload student work to detectors.
Guelph's fall 2026 guidance prohibits using AI detection tools to support
academic integrity allegations and retains human responsibility for substantive
academic evaluation.

These are separate institutional statements. Advice against reliance on a
detector, a restriction on uploading student work, and a prohibition on using
detectors to support allegations should not be collapsed into one universal rule.

Sources: [NTHU teaching and learning guidance](https://ctld.site.nthu.edu.tw/p/412-1217-20815.php?Lang=zh-tw),
[Penn teaching guidance](https://cetli.upenn.edu/resources/generative-ai/),
[Guelph provisional recommendations](https://otl.uoguelph.ca/teaching-assessment-resources/teaching-context-ai/provisional-recommendations-use-generative-ai).

### Tool access remains conditional on the user and academic context

Queen's University Belfast's recorded guidance lists institution-authenticated
Copilot Chat and Google AI services for staff and students. Penn's additions
include licensed AI services. Toronto's recorded AI Kitchen evidence lists
ChatGPT Edu for staff, faculty, and librarians with limited seats and negotiated
pricing; this does not establish access for every student.

UNSW College's Mentor AI statement is scoped to English Language Courses
(ELICOS), rather than all UNSW courses. VU Amsterdam's guidance restricts
requiring unlicensed applications and retains conditions on institutional login
and sensitive data. Access to a university-supported tool does not, by itself,
grant permission to use it in every assessment.

Sources: [Queen's DigiHub](https://blogs.qub.ac.uk/digitallearning/ai/understanding-ai/using-ai/),
[Penn AI tools](https://isc.upenn.edu/ai/tools/penn-generative-ai-tools-resources),
[Toronto AI Kitchen](https://ai.utoronto.ca/ai-kitchen/),
[UNSW College student guidelines](https://my.unswcollege.edu.au/student-support/student-ai-guidelines),
[VU student examinations guidance](https://vu.nl/en/student/examinations/generative-ai-your-use-our-expectations).

### Data approval depends on the tool, contract, and institutional classification

Columbia's CUIMC record concerns an AI tools and data classification matrix
for the medical center; central tool availability does not establish approval
for sensitive medical-center data. Oregon's recorded service guidance
distinguishes tools approved for Red, Amber, and Green data from tools limited
to Green data. WashU's recorded tools guidance distinguishes general university
data from protected health information and notes that some enterprise tools
are not approved for the latter.

Waterloo's teaching guidance also addresses student work, course materials,
and student information in tools that do not meet institutional security
requirements. These classifications belong to their respective institutions;
their labels are not interchangeable across universities.

Sources: [CUIMC AI guidance](https://www.it.cuimc.columbia.edu/ai-and-generative-technology-use-cuimc),
[Oregon AI service offerings](https://service.uoregon.edu/TDClient/2030/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=140911),
[WashU tools and infrastructure](https://ai.washu.edu/tools/),
[Waterloo academic guidance](https://uwaterloo.ca/associate-vice-president-academic/artificial-intelligence-uw).

### Agentic tools and course disclosure appear in the added records

UCI's recorded security guidance includes agentic AI alongside chatbots and
addresses system data access. USNH's recorded platform guidance includes
DeepThought for technical workflows and autonomous agents. The Universidad
Adolfo Ibáñez policy record includes governance of agentic systems and research
disclosure. Lund's education guidelines require course restrictions and
reporting requirements for generative AI to be communicated consistently.

Together, these additions illustrate several governance questions beyond
whether a chatbot is available. They are a bounded set of examples, not evidence
of a measured global trend or a ranking of policy maturity.

Sources: [UCI AI security guidance](https://www.security.uci.edu/how-to/ai-chatbot/),
[USNH approved platforms](https://td.usnh.edu/TDClient/60/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=5322),
[Universidad Adolfo Ibáñez academic AI guidance](https://www.uai.cl/cuerpo-academico/desarrollo-academico/artificial-intelligence-for-academia),
[Lund AI guidance](https://www.staff.lu.se/support-and-tools/it-mail-and-telephony/ai-lund-university).

## Evidence and monitoring boundary

Only artifacts included in the selected public release contribute to this
report. Unpublished research candidates, local page redesigns, artwork, and
monitoring-only signals are excluded from the policy findings and dataset
totals. Search visibility and onsite engagement are separate from policy
evidence and are not metrics in this report.

Original-language evidence remains canonical; display translations are helper
text. Official university sources remain authoritative for institutional
language. This report summarizes the selected dataset snapshot and does not
replace current course instructions, tool terms, or institutional guidance.
It does not provide legal, academic integrity, or course-permission advice.
